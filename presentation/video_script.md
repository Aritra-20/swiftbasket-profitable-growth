# Video script (about 4:16)

Read it slowly and naturally. "..." means take a short breath. The same text is in each slide's speaker notes.

Built on storytelling-with-data practice: say the big idea first (slide 1), build through three leaks (customers, delivery, stock), reveal what the random test proved, end on the actions and a one-line answer to the COO.

About 600 words: about 4:16 at 140 words a minute, or 4:36 at a slower 130. The appendix slide (10) is for questions only; don't show it in the video.

| Time | Slide | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | 1. Cover · the big idea | Hi, I'm Aritra Pal from Great Lakes, Chennai. SwiftBasket's COO asked: we're growing, but which growth is worth having? My answer in one line... we're paying for growth we don't need. Discounts and refunds take sixty-six rupees out of every order, and we keep forty-four. The fix: discount only where it changes behaviour, and stop the leaks in delivery and stock. |
| 0:26 | 2. Lean in / pull back | Ms. Nair had two hunches. One... our busiest customers may be our least profitable. Half right: they earn the least per order, but they're still worth the most in total. Two... our fastest-growing stores may be leaking money. No: they earn more than average. The real leak is nine stores with poor stock records. So here's where to lean in, and where to pull back. |
| 0:54 | 3. Business health | First, the baseline. Orders are almost flat, about two and a half thousand a month. Follow one average order: about a hundred and sixty-seven rupees comes in, discounts take forty-five, refunds twenty-one, and after picking and delivery we keep forty-four. One order in five loses money. So watch margin per order, not order count. That margin leaks in three places: customers, delivery and stock. |
| 1:21 | 4. Customers | Start with customers. The discount doesn't grow with the basket: it's about forty-five rupees whether the basket is small or large. So orders under four hundred rupees lose about ten rupees each. And customers who lean on discounts order more than twice as often, but earn us twenty rupees an order instead of seventy. That's growth that looks good and isn't. |
| 1:47 | 5. Delivery | The second leak is delivery. Twenty, twenty-five and thirty-minute promises go to customers at the same distance, and delivery takes about nineteen minutes either way. So the promise, not the rider, decides whether we're late. A twenty-minute promise beyond four kilometres is broken seventy-two percent of the time, and late orders get lower ratings. It's a real risk: Outlook Business reported in January that Blinkit and Zepto agreed to drop their ten-minute claims over rider safety. |
| 2:20 | 6. Stores | The third leak is stock. Two in three delivered orders hit a stock-out. With none, there's no refund; with one, the refund averages twenty-eight rupees and ratings fall. The nine stores with the worst stock records earn about eleven rupees less per order than the best. This one gets fixed inside the store. |
| 2:42 | 7. Promo test | Now, do discounts work? The Q1 promo was a random test, so we can see what the offer really changed. Customers who got one ordered a bit more, but each gave us twelve and a half rupees less margin: many would have bought anyway. For recent customers, no offer paid back. The one clear win was free delivery for dormant customers, with no orders last year: about seventeen rupees each. Their records have a data quirk, so I'd re-test it first. |
| 3:17 | 8. Three actions | So, three actions for next quarter. One... spend discounts only where they pay: free delivery for dormant customers, and no discounts under a four-hundred-rupee basket. Two... set the promise by distance: twenty-five minutes within two kilometres, thirty beyond. Late orders fall from seventeen percent to about seven and a half. Three... fix stock records in the nine weakest stores, worth up to three percent more margin. Each starts as a small test, so we learn before we scale. |
| 3:51 | 9. How I worked · close | On method: I checked the data first, used only fields known at decision time, and judged everything on margin, not orders. AI helped me write the code and draft the wording, and I re-checked every number from the raw data. So, Ms. Nair: lean in where growth pays, and pull back where we're paying for orders we'd get anyway. Thank you. |
